## 3. Is this a new program or an existing program?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad runs as connected levels. (1) Launchpad Foundations — an entry summer program in core AI and technical skills. (2) Launchpad 101 — a yearlong, learn-and-earn program for high school juniors and seniors, four afternoons a week with summer intensives, where students earn stipends, complete paid internships, and earn industry certifications (e.g. Certiport Generative AI Foundations); it offers two pathways, Software Development and Entrepreneurial Leadership, so students not drawn to coding still build real AI/STEM skills. (3) LiftOff — a six-month, full-time, AI-first software-development track for graduates ready to go deeper (participants 18–24), with a guaranteed $6,000 stipend floor over six months plus wraparound supports (transportation, food, a laptop, and coaching). (4) Launchpad Inc. — a nonprofit social enterprise, now the second half of LiftOff, that puts participants on paid client projects at $20/hour for local nonprofits, foundations, and small businesses. Participants train against Launchpad's AI Engineer competency framework — six competencies and 24 skills, staged Associate → AI Engineer (Junior is the graduation bar) — assessed on deployed work rather than seat time and tracked in Beacon, Building 21's competency and data platform.

## 7. Describe the program in detail: What problem or opportunity does your program address? Who are the main clients of this program? What specific interventions will this program deliver? How will this program help people in the Philadelphia area find, keep or improve current and future STEM-related fields?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad recruits Philadelphia young people from more than 30 non-selective high schools and across 30+ ZIP codes, no prior experience required, and pays them from day one so finishing is financially possible. The advanced phases most funders support are LiftOff — six months of full-time, AI-first software-development training for participants 18–24 — and Launchpad Inc., a nonprofit social enterprise that puts participants on real, paid client projects ($20/hour) building automations, dashboards, integrations, and agents for Philadelphia nonprofits and small businesses. Participants train against the AI Engineer competency framework (six competencies, 24 skills; prompting and context engineering, building automations and agents, connecting systems, structuring data, evaluating AI output, and managing cost and risk), earn industry certifications (Generative AI Foundations, PCEP), and are assessed on deployed work tracked in Beacon, so every graduate finishes with a portfolio of shipped AI systems and the client references early-career tech hiring now screens on. Wraparound supports — a guaranteed $6,000 stipend floor, transportation, food, a laptop, and Career Success Coaching that continues through the first year of employment — remove the barriers that otherwise force low-income young adults to drop out. Delivered at no cost from Launchpad's Center City hub at 801 Market Street.

## 8. How will you ensure that this program drives economic mobility? What barriers to access does this program aim to remove, and how? What steps have you taken to make your services more accessible? Are there groups you find it difficult to connect with, and how will you address this?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Equity is the design. Launchpad recruits from non-selective Philadelphia high schools that do not offer computer science, requires no prior experience, and pays participants so access to a high-paying tech career does not depend on which school a student attended or whether they can afford to train unpaid. The service population is about 85% Black or Brown, nearly 90% low-income, and 100% free/reduced-lunch eligible. Because fewer than 10% of recruited students are initially drawn to software engineering, Launchpad built the Entrepreneurial Leadership pathway so the other ~90% — including many young women who do not see themselves in a coding-only program — reach the same AI skills, paid projects, and credentials. Reaching young women in software specifically remains an active recruitment focus.

## 9. Is this a joint funding proposal?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad works with 17–20 employer partners who inform the curriculum, host paid placements, sit on the advisory board, and run mock interviews and portfolio reviews — including Accenture (hosted the Cohort 1 graduation), Seer Interactive (hired a graduate over 200+ applicants, then more), Bentley Systems, CreateAccess (converted an intern to a permanent role), Hack Club, HiTouch Enterprises, AECOM, Nerd Street, PGW, and PECO. Through Launchpad Inc., participants have delivered paid client work for Philadelphia nonprofits and civic organizations including North10 Philadelphia, the Share Food Program, and Rock to the Future. Launchpad partners with more than 30 Philadelphia high schools for recruitment, relies on parent organization Building 21 for the competency-based model and the Beacon platform, and has a dual-enrollment partnership with Mastery Charter School. A standing committee of industry professionals shapes the curriculum to what employers hire for.
