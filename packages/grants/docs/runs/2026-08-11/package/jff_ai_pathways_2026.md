## 1. Project Overview and Innovation: Describe the challenge or opportunity facing early-career workers that your proposed project seeks to address. Why is this issue important in the context of an AI-driven economy? What is your proposed idea, and what makes it additive to existing approaches?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad's innovation is a paid on-ramp that produces working professionals rather than a class that ends at a job fair. First, the AI Engineer competency framework: rather than teaching tools that are obsolete within a year, Launchpad defined the durable capabilities of an AI builder as six competencies and 24 skills, staged Associate → AI Engineer, each assessed against a shipped portfolio artifact, not a test score. The framework is versioned (now in its fifth version), reviewed by a standing curriculum committee, and openly licensed for other programs to adopt. Second, paid work from day one through Launchpad Inc., a real social enterprise: participants deploy AI systems for Philadelphia organizations that could not otherwise afford them, producing the deployed work and client references early-career tech hiring now demands while removing the financial barrier. Third, an honest redefinition of success — earning power over time toward a $50,000 living wage — which lets programs serving the hardest-to-place populations show real progress instead of failing a metric built for a labor market that no longer exists. If proven at scale, the model is transferable to other cities and employer markets.

## 4. Early-Career Impact: Who are the early-career workers your idea seeks to benefit? How does your proposed work aim to improve access, job quality, skill development, or career mobility in an AI-transformed economy?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad serves Philadelphia young people ages 16–24: high school juniors and seniors (roughly 16–18) in Launchpad 101, and recent graduates / young adults (18–24) in LiftOff and Launchpad Inc. Participants come almost entirely from non-selective Philadelphia high schools across 30+ ZIP codes; all qualify for free or reduced-price lunch, nearly 90% are from low-income households, and about 85% are Black or Brown. No prior coding experience is required. (Funders that require an 18–24 band are served by the LiftOff/Inc profile; Launchpad 101 reaches the 16–18 group.)

## 5. How will you assess whether your project is making a difference for the early-career workers you aim to serve? What outcomes or indicators would show progress toward your goals?

*Ready to review — no stated limit.*

> Launchpad measures success as earning power over time toward a $50,000 living wage, not a job on graduation day, and tracks participants at six and twelve months. Results to date: LiftOff Cohort 1 — 12 graduates (Oct 2025); at six months 11 of 12 (92%) were in paid work or training, roughly three-quarters of it contract work through Launchpad Inc., averaging about $15,000 annualized at six months and projected toward ~$35,000 at nine months; 100% are in paid work now (most recent status); alumni have earned $229,000+ in documented wages. Notable placements: four graduates hired at Seer Interactive after out-competing 200+ applicants, plus roles at Bentley Systems, a Hack Club fellowship, and a promotion at CreateAccess. Since 2023, Launchpad has paid $350,268 directly to 45 participants across 88 jobs (most recent precise figure, William Penn Jul 2026; the GSK Aug-7 draft rounds this to '$500,000+' — align when finalizing GSK). Launchpad 101: ~72% completion in the most recent program year; 95% of graduates move directly into college, further training, or work; PCEP Python pass rates 70–100% per cohort (100% most recent). Twelve-month retention data arrives in fall 2026.
