## 4. Tell us your organization's main purpose.

Launchpad builds accelerated pathways that connect Philadelphia young adults to high-paying, future-ready tech careers. Through a paid AI-engineering program grounded in real client work, participants who arrive with a high school diploma and little else gain applied AI skills, industry certifications, a portfolio of deployed work, and the paid experience employers now demand, at no cost to them, on a path toward a $50,000 living wage.

## 5. Briefly describe your organization's main services or work areas.

Launchpad runs as four connected phases, each with its own entry point.

Foundations is an entry summer program in core AI and technical skills. 146 students have completed it.

Launchpad 101 is a yearlong learn-and-earn program for high school juniors and seniors, four afternoons a week with summer intensives. Students earn stipends, complete paid internships, and sit for industry certifications. It runs two pathways, Software Development and Entrepreneurial Leadership, so a student who is not drawn to coding still builds real AI and STEM skills. 38 students have completed it.

Lightspeed is an accelerated phase between 101 and LiftOff. 15 students have completed it.

LiftOff is a six-month, full-time, AI-first software development track for participants 18 to 24, with a guaranteed $6,000 stipend floor over six months plus transportation, food, a laptop, and coaching. 12 have completed it and 18 are enrolled now. Its second half runs through Launchpad Inc., a nonprofit social enterprise that places participants on paid client projects for local nonprofits, foundations, and small businesses.

Participants train against Launchpad's AI Engineer competency framework, six competencies and 24 skills staged from Associate to AI Engineer, with Junior as the graduation bar. Assessment is on deployed work rather than seat time, tracked in Beacon, Building 21's competency and data platform.

## 7. Describe the program in detail: What problem or opportunity does your program address? Who are the main clients of this program? What specific interventions will this program deliver? How will this program help people in the Philadelphia area find, keep or improve current and future STEM-related fields?

Entry-level software work is the rung AI removed first, and the young people Launchpad recruits were reaching for exactly that rung. Launchpad's answer is to stop selling training and start selling delivered work: participants ship AI systems for paying clients before they graduate, so they apply with a portfolio and client references rather than a certificate.

The main clients are Philadelphia young people 16 to 24 recruited from non-selective high schools across more than 30 ZIP codes, with no prior experience required. 301 have enrolled to date.

The intervention has three parts. Participants are paid from day one, through a $6,000 stipend floor in LiftOff and hourly wages on client projects through Launchpad Inc., so finishing does not depend on being able to afford six unpaid months. They train against the AI Engineer competency framework, six competencies and 24 skills covering prompting and context engineering, building automations and agents, connecting systems, structuring data, evaluating AI output, and managing cost and risk, each evidenced by a shipped artifact in Beacon rather than a test score. And they sit for industry certifications, currently Certiport Generative AI Foundations and PCEP.

The results so far are earnings, not enrollment. Launchpad has paid $367,662 directly to 45 participants across 86 jobs since 2023, at an average of $15.52 an hour and 20.3 hours a week. Currently active jobs average $19.28 an hour, which is the direction that matters. Participants have held paid technical roles at Accenture, Seer Interactive, CreateAccess, Bentley Systems, HiTouch Enterprise, Hack Club, AECOM, and Nerd Street.

## 8. How will you ensure that this program drives economic mobility? What barriers to access does this program aim to remove, and how? What steps have you taken to make your services more accessible? Are there groups you find it difficult to connect with, and how will you address this?

Access is the design, not a program add-on. Launchpad recruits from non-selective Philadelphia high schools that do not offer computer science, requires no prior experience, and pays participants, so reaching a high-paying tech career does not depend on which school a student got into or whether their family can absorb six months of unpaid training. Transportation, food, and a laptop are provided, because each of those is a common reason a low-income participant stops attending.

Of the 301 young people enrolled to date, 289 identify as a race or ethnicity other than white alone. `[STAFF: 96.0% on that definition, or 80.4% counting Black or Latino only. Pick the population GSK means before filing.]`

Fewer than 10% of recruited students arrive drawn to software engineering. Launchpad built the Entrepreneurial Leadership pathway so the rest reach the same AI skills, paid projects, and credentials without having to want a developer job first. Recruiting young women into the software pathway specifically remains the hardest gap and an active focus.
