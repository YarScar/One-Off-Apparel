## 7. Timeframe of Evaluation Results

Launchpad assesses participants on deployed work against the AI Engineer competency framework, six competencies and 24 skills, with each skill evidenced by a portfolio artifact such as a shipped integration or a system a client confirmed. Assessment is tracked continuously in Beacon, Building 21's competency platform, rather than by seat time or a single test, so competency results are available at any point in a cohort.

The headline outcome, the share of graduates in paid work or training and their average annualized earnings against the $50,000 living-wage target, is measured at six months and again at twelve months after graduation. ## 9. Investment Area Alignment (3,000 characters)

Primary investment area: career learning.

Launchpad is the youth workforce development program of Building 21, and the Trust's career learning criteria describe what it does rather than something adjacent to it. Launchpad recruits from non-selective Philadelphia high schools that do not offer computer science, across more than 30 ZIP codes, and requires no prior experience. 301 young people have enrolled since 2023. Of those, 289 identify as a race or ethnicity other than white alone.

Career preparation here is not exploration followed by a referral. High school juniors and seniors in Launchpad 101 spend a year in a learn-and-earn program, four afternoons a week with summer intensives, earning stipends and completing paid internships while sitting for industry certifications. 38 students have completed it. Graduates who want to go further enter Lightspeed and then LiftOff, a six-month full-time AI engineering track for 18 to 24 year olds whose second half places participants on paid client projects through Launchpad Inc., a nonprofit social enterprise serving Philadelphia nonprofits and small businesses. 15 students have completed Lightspeed, 12 have completed LiftOff, and 18 are in LiftOff now.

The mechanism that distinguishes this from career exposure is that participants are paid from the first day and are assessed on work that shipped. Six competencies and 24 skills in the AI Engineer framework are each evidenced by a portfolio artifact tracked in Beacon, so a graduate leaves with deployed systems and client references rather than a certificate of attendance. Since 2023, Launchpad has paid $367,662 directly to 45 participants across 86 jobs, averaging $15.52 an hour, and $19.28 an hour across currently active jobs. Participants have held paid technical roles at Accenture, Seer Interactive, CreateAccess, Bentley Systems, HiTouch Enterprise, Hack Club, AECOM, and Nerd Street.

Secondary alignment: academic learning and educational support. The competency framework was built by Building 21 as a standards-aligned assessment model, and the wraparound supports that keep participants enrolled, transportation, food, a laptop, and coaching that continues through the first year of employment, are the reason completion is possible for a student whose family cannot absorb unpaid training time.
