## 1. Project Overview and Innovation: Describe the challenge or opportunity facing early-career workers that your proposed project seeks to address. Why is this issue important in the context of an AI-driven economy? What is your proposed idea, and what makes it additive to existing approaches?

AI removed the bottom rung first. The entry-level developer role that a bootcamp graduate could reach in 2021 now asks for shipped work and references, which is precisely what a young person without a network cannot get. Training programs kept optimizing for a placement that no longer exists at the other end.

Launchpad's answer is to make the paid work part of the program rather than the reward for finishing it. Three parts make it additive.

First, the AI Engineer competency framework. Rather than teaching tools that are obsolete within a year, Launchpad defined the durable capabilities of an AI builder as six competencies and 24 skills, staged from Associate to AI Engineer, each assessed against a shipped portfolio artifact rather than a test score. The framework is on its fifth version, reviewed by a standing curriculum committee, and openly licensed for other programs to adopt.

Second, paid work from day one through Launchpad Inc., a nonprofit social enterprise. Participants deploy AI systems for Philadelphia organizations that could not otherwise afford them. This produces the deployed work and client references early-career technical hiring screens on, and it removes the financial reason a low-income participant would quit. Since 2023 Launchpad has paid $367,662 directly to 45 participants across 86 jobs.

Third, success measured as earning power over time toward a $50,000 living wage, checked at six and twelve months, rather than a job on graduation day. That lets a program serving the hardest-to-place young people show real progress instead of failing against a metric built for a labor market that is gone.

## 2. Operational Readiness: Outline your high-level milestones for the grant period. What do you expect to design, test, or learn by the end of the grant period?

Milestones follow one full LiftOff and Launchpad Inc. cycle.

Recruitment and enrollment. Classroom-intensive phase, roughly weeks 1 to 12, stipend-supported at a $6,000 floor over the six months. Transition to paid client work through Launchpad Inc. for the second half. Graduation with a Beacon-tracked portfolio and industry certifications. Earnings checks at six and twelve months after graduation.

By the end of the grant period Launchpad expects to know two things it does not know now: whether the paid-client-work half of LiftOff produces better twelve-month earnings than the classroom half predicts, and which of the 24 competency skills actually correlate with a participant being retained past twelve months. The first twelve-month cohort data arrives in fall 2026.

Responsibilities: the Executive Director owns strategic direction. Program directors, technical instructors, and coaches run delivery. Launchpad Inc. delivery leads supervise client projects. Building 21 provides finance, compliance, and platform support.

## 4. Early-Career Impact: Who are the early-career workers your idea seeks to benefit? How does your proposed work aim to improve access, job quality, skill development, or career mobility in an AI-transformed economy?

Launchpad serves Philadelphia young people 16 to 24: high school juniors and seniors in Launchpad 101, and recent graduates and young adults 18 to 24 in LiftOff and Launchpad Inc. 301 have enrolled since 2023. They come almost entirely from non-selective Philadelphia high schools across more than 30 ZIP codes, and no prior coding experience is required. Of the 301, 289 identify as a race or ethnicity other than white alone. `[STAFF: 96.0% on that definition, 80.4% counting Black or Latino only.]`

Job quality is the part most easily skipped. Participants earn $15.52 an hour on average across all jobs held since 2023, and $19.28 an hour across jobs active now, working roughly 20 hours a week. The point of the paid client work is that it pays above the stipend and produces evidence an employer will read, which is what moves a participant from a job to a career.

For a funder requiring an 18 to 24 band, the LiftOff and Launchpad Inc. profile is the relevant one. Launchpad 101 reaches the 16 to 18 group.

## 6. Learning and Scaling Potential: What are the key questions you hope to explore or learn through this project? Please describe how you plan to capture and share measures of impact and/or learnings during or after the grant period. If your pilot is successful, how might you envision scaling it? What key resources or support would help make that possible?

Three questions drive the work. Does paid client work produce better twelve-month earnings than classroom training of the same length? Which of the 24 competency skills predict retention past twelve months? And can a social enterprise generate enough client revenue to cover a meaningful share of participant wages, which is what would make the model affordable to replicate?

Capture is already built. Every competency assessment, portfolio artifact, enrollment record, and job with its wage and hours is recorded in Beacon and in Launchpad's data platform, so cohort-level reporting is a query rather than a data-collection project. The AI Engineer framework is versioned and openly licensed, and the same is intended for the assessment rubrics and the client-project intake process, which are the two pieces another city would need.

Scaling means a second market running LiftOff and a local Launchpad Inc. against the same framework. What that requires is a client pipeline in the new market, delivery supervision capable of shipping client work to a professional standard, and stipend capital for the classroom half, since client revenue only covers the second half.
